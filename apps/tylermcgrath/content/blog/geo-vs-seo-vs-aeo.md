SEO, AEO and GEO are three layers of the same job. **SEO** (search engine optimization) earns your pages a ranking in search results. **AEO** (answer engine optimization) earns your content the direct answer to a specific question. **GEO** (generative engine optimization) earns your brand a citation or mention inside an AI-generated answer. They share a foundation, but each optimizes for a different outcome and needs a different measurement.

The acronyms get thrown around as if they were rival strategies. They are not. In this guide I break down what each discipline covers, where they overlap, where they genuinely diverge, and how to decide where your time and budget should go.

## The Short Answer: Three Outcomes, One Foundation

The difference between SEO, AEO and GEO is the outcome each one targets. SEO targets a position, AEO targets an answer, and GEO targets representation inside a synthesized response.

A useful way to picture it is a single search journey:

1. A user types a question into Google. SEO determines whether your page appears in the results.
2. Google shows a featured snippet or a short answer at the top. AEO determines whether that answer is yours.
3. Google shows an AI Overview, or the user asks the same thing in ChatGPT or Perplexity. GEO determines whether your brand is cited or named in that generated answer.

All three outcomes depend on the same underlying assets: pages that can be crawled and indexed, content that genuinely answers the question, clear signals about who you are, and a reputation that other sources confirm. That is why I treat them as layers rather than silos.

## What SEO Covers

**Search engine optimization** is the practice of improving a website so it ranks higher in search engine results for relevant queries and earns more qualified organic traffic.

SEO has three traditional components:

- **Technical SEO.** Crawlability, indexation, site speed, mobile usability, canonicalization, internal linking and structured data. The baseline rules are in [Google Search Central documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs).
- **On-page and content SEO.** Matching content to search intent, covering topics thoroughly, writing useful titles and headings, and building topical depth.
- **Off-page SEO.** Earning links and mentions from other reputable sites, which signal authority and help discovery.

SEO's unit of value is the page. A page ranks, a page earns the click, and a page converts. Success is measured with rankings, impressions, click-through rate, organic sessions and the conversions those sessions produce.

SEO is not going away. It is the precondition for most AI visibility, because the AI systems that search the web pull from the same index-quality pages that rank.

## What AEO Covers

**Answer engine optimization** is the practice of structuring content so that search engines and assistants can extract it as the direct answer to a question. The goal is to be the answer, not just a result.

AEO emerged around featured snippets, People Also Ask boxes and voice assistants, where a single response is read aloud or displayed above the links. It now extends to AI summaries, which also need concise, extractable answers.

AEO work focuses on:

- Researching the specific questions your audience asks.
- Leading sections with a direct, 40 to 60 word answer.
- Using the format that fits the question: a definition, a numbered list of steps, a comparison table or a single figure.
- Writing headings that mirror the question.
- Adding structured data that clarifies what each page is.

AEO's unit of value is the individual answer. One page might own several answers for related questions. I go through the full process in the [answer engine optimization guide](/blog/answer-engine-optimization-guide).

## What GEO Covers

**Generative engine optimization** is the practice of improving how often and how accurately AI systems such as ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot and Claude cite or mention your brand in the answers they generate.

GEO goes beyond formatting answers. Generative engines synthesize information from many sources, so GEO also has to address:

- **Brand representation.** What does an AI system say when someone asks about your company or category?
- **Entity clarity.** Can the model distinguish your brand from similarly named companies and connect it to the right products, people and places?
- **Corroboration.** Do reviews, publications, directories and communities describe you consistently?
- **Crawler access.** Have you allowed the specific AI search crawlers that fetch content for live answers?
- **Prompt-level measurement.** Are you tracking how answers change over time for the prompts that matter?

GEO's unit of value is the passage, fact or mention. Your brand might be named in an answer without any link, and that still counts. For a full introduction, see [what is generative engine optimization](/blog/what-is-generative-engine-optimization).

## GEO vs SEO vs AEO: Side-by-Side Comparison

The clearest way to see the differences is to compare the three disciplines across the same dimensions. The table below is the summary I use when explaining this to leadership teams.

| Dimension | SEO | AEO | GEO |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Core question | Will my page rank? | Will my content be the answer? | Will AI cite or mention my brand? |
| Primary surfaces | Organic search results | Featured snippets, People Also Ask, voice | ChatGPT, AI Overviews, AI Mode, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot, Claude |
| Unit of value | Page | Answer block | Passage, fact, brand mention |
| Typical query | Keyword or short phrase | Explicit question | Conversational, multi-part prompt |
| Key content trait | Comprehensive, intent-matched | Concise, extractable, formatted | Self-contained, specific, corroborated |
| Off-site emphasis | Backlinks | Moderate | Mentions, reviews, publications, consensus |
| Main metrics | Rankings, clicks, conversions | Snippet and answer ownership | Citation share, mention rate, answer accuracy |
| Click outcome | Click is the goal | Click is optional | Click is a bonus |

The bottom row is the one that changes how you report. In SEO a ranking without a click is a missed opportunity. In AEO and GEO, being the answer can be the win, even if the user never visits.

## Where SEO, AEO and GEO Overlap

SEO, AEO and GEO overlap on everything that makes content findable, trustworthy and easy to understand. In practice, a large share of the work is identical.

The shared foundation includes:

- **Crawlability and indexation.** If search engines and AI crawlers cannot reach a page, none of the three disciplines can use it.
- **Helpful, people-first content.** Content written to genuinely solve a problem is what ranks, what gets extracted and what gets cited.
- **Clear structure.** Descriptive headings, short paragraphs, lists and tables help ranking algorithms, snippet extraction and language models alike.
- **Structured data.** Schema markup clarifies entities and page types for all three.
- **Authority and trust.** Expert authors, accurate information and a strong reputation help in all three. I cover this in [E-E-A-T in the age of AI search](/blog/eeat-in-the-ai-era).

This overlap is good news. It means you do not need three teams or three content libraries. One well-built page can rank, own a snippet and get cited in an AI answer at the same time.

## Where the Three Genuinely Diverge

The three disciplines diverge in query style, content granularity, off-site emphasis and measurement. These differences are why GEO and AEO deserve explicit attention rather than being assumed into SEO.

### Query style

SEO keyword research tends to focus on short head and mid-tail terms with measurable search volume. AEO focuses on explicit questions. GEO deals with long, conversational prompts that often combine constraints, such as "what's the best accounting software for a nonprofit with two staff that integrates with our donor CRM." Those prompts rarely appear in keyword tools, so GEO research leans on sales calls, support tickets and community discussions.

### Content granularity

SEO can succeed with a page that is strong overall even if no single paragraph is quotable. AEO and GEO need individual passages that make sense when lifted out of context. A paragraph that starts with "As mentioned above, it also..." is fine for a human reading top to bottom, but weak for extraction.

### Off-site emphasis

SEO has historically focused on links. GEO cares about links too, but also about unlinked brand mentions, review content and how consistently third parties describe you. When a generative engine summarizes "the best options," it often draws on lists, reviews and discussions on other sites, not just your own pages.

### Measurement

SEO has mature tooling: rank trackers, Search Console and analytics. AEO can be tracked with rank tools that report snippet ownership. GEO measurement is newer and noisier, because AI answers vary by phrasing, session and location. It relies on repeated prompt testing and AI referral segments. The [AI search visibility measurement guide](/blog/measuring-ai-search-visibility) covers how to make that data reliable enough to act on.

## The Other Acronyms: LLMO, AIO, AI SEO and SXO

You will see several other labels used for overlapping ideas. Knowing them prevents confusion when agencies or tools use different terms for the same work.

- **LLMO (large language model optimization)** is effectively a synonym for GEO, emphasizing the model rather than the search engine.
- **AIO (AI optimization)** is a broad umbrella some marketers use for any optimization aimed at AI systems. Note that "AIO" is also sometimes used as shorthand for AI Overviews, which adds confusion.
- **AI SEO** usually means either GEO or the use of AI tools to do SEO work. Ask which one someone means.
- **SXO (search experience optimization)** combines SEO with user experience and conversion optimization. It predates AI search but remains useful.
- **GSO (generative search optimization)** is another variant name for GEO.

My advice is not to argue about labels. Ask what outcome the work targets and how it will be measured. If the answer is "citations and mentions in AI answers," it is GEO, whatever the slide calls it.

## How to Prioritize SEO, AEO and GEO by Business Type

The right balance depends on how your customers search and how they buy. SEO is always the base layer, but the emphasis on AEO and GEO shifts by business model.

| Business type | SEO emphasis | AEO emphasis | GEO emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Local service business | High: local SEO and listings | Medium: service and pricing questions | Medium: accurate AI descriptions and reviews |
| B2B SaaS | High: product, comparison and use-case pages | High: definitional and how-to content | High: "best tool for" and comparison prompts |
| Ecommerce | High: category and product pages | Medium: buying-guide questions | Medium: product recommendations and reviews |
| Publisher or media | High: topical authority and freshness | High: news and explainer answers | High: citation in AI answers |
| Professional services | High: expertise and service pages | High: common client questions | High: reputation and expert positioning |

A local plumber needs Google Business Profile, reviews and a clear service-area page before worrying about prompt testing. A B2B software company competing in a crowded category may find that AI answers already shape its shortlists, which makes GEO a near-term priority.

## A Unified Workflow for All Three

The most efficient approach is a single workflow that produces SEO, AEO and GEO value from the same effort. Here is the sequence I recommend.

1. **Research topics, questions and prompts together.** Start with keyword research for demand, add question research for AEO, and add conversational prompts from customer conversations for GEO.
2. **Assign one owner page per topic.** Each priority topic gets one page designed to be the definitive answer, avoiding cannibalization.
3. **Build the page answer-first.** Open with a direct answer, then expand. Make each section self-contained with its own lead sentence.
4. **Choose formats deliberately.** Use numbered steps for processes, tables for comparisons and definitions for terms.
5. **Add structured data and author signals.** Mark up the page type, organization and author, and show real credentials.
6. **Confirm crawler access.** Verify that search engines and the AI crawlers you want can fetch the page.
7. **Earn corroboration.** Pursue mentions, reviews and links that confirm the page's claims and the brand's expertise.
8. **Measure all three layers.** Track rankings and clicks, snippet ownership, and AI citations for the same topic in one report.

When I run this workflow for a site, the reporting is what changes most. Instead of three separate dashboards, each topic gets one row showing its rank, whether it owns the answer, and whether AI engines cite it.

## Mistakes When Splitting the Disciplines Apart

The biggest mistakes come from treating SEO, AEO and GEO as competing priorities rather than layers. These are the ones I see most often.

- **Abandoning SEO for "AI optimization."** Without crawlable, indexed, authoritative pages, there is little for AI engines to cite.
- **Creating separate "AI pages."** Duplicating content for AI systems splits authority and confuses both readers and crawlers.
- **Reporting only traffic.** If AI answers reduce clicks but increase brand mentions, a traffic-only report will hide the real picture.
- **Optimizing format but not substance.** Answer-first formatting cannot rescue content that has nothing original or specific to say.
- **Ignoring off-site reputation.** SEO teams often stop at the website boundary, while GEO outcomes depend heavily on what other sources say.
- **Letting acronyms drive org charts.** Hiring a "GEO team" that does not coordinate with SEO and content usually produces duplicated, conflicting work.

## Bringing SEO, AEO and GEO Together

SEO gets you found, AEO gets you chosen as the answer, and GEO gets you cited and represented accurately inside AI-generated responses. They are not alternatives. They are successive layers built on the same foundation of accessible, helpful, trustworthy content.

The practical move is to keep SEO as the base, adopt AEO formatting as a content standard, and add GEO measurement so you can see how AI systems describe you.
