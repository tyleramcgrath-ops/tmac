Local SEO in the AI search era is the work of making your business the clear, consistent and trusted answer whenever someone asks Google, ChatGPT, Perplexity or another assistant for a business like yours nearby. It rests on the same pillars as before — Google Business Profile, reviews, citations and a strong website — but now those signals are summarized into a recommendation instead of a list of ten links.

That change raises the stakes. In a classic local pack, a customer might scroll past three businesses and still find you. In an AI answer, the assistant may name two or three businesses with a sentence about why, and the rest simply do not appear. This guide covers how local recommendations get assembled and the practical work that puts your business into them.

## How Local Search Changed When AI Started Answering

Local search used to be a map and a list. A person typed "emergency plumber near me" and saw a local pack, organic results and ads. They compared star ratings themselves.

Now that same person might ask a conversational question: "Who's a reliable emergency plumber in Northside that won't overcharge?" Google may show an AI Overview alongside the local pack. ChatGPT or Perplexity may return a short list of named businesses with summaries drawn from reviews and websites. Voice assistants may give a single answer.

**AI local search** is any experience where an AI system interprets a location-based need and responds with specific business recommendations, often with reasons attached. What makes it different is synthesis: the assistant reads many sources and condenses them into a judgment.

Three things follow from that:

1. **Specific queries reward specific information.** "Won't overcharge" or "good with anxious kids" are things AI can answer only if reviews and web pages actually discuss them.
2. **Consistency becomes credibility.** If your hours differ across three sites, an assistant has reason to hedge or skip you.
3. **Your reputation gets paraphrased.** The assistant describes you in its own words, based on what others have written.

For the broader shift in how AI engines choose sources, see the guide to [generative engine optimization](/blog/what-is-generative-engine-optimization). The rest of this post is the local playbook.

## Where AI Assistants Get Local Business Information

AI systems build local recommendations from a mix of business data, reviews, websites and third-party mentions. The exact sources and weights are not publicly documented for most assistants, so the practical approach is to be accurate and well-represented in every major source.

| Source type | Examples | What it contributes | Your control |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google business data | Google Business Profile, Maps | Name, category, hours, location, reviews, photos | High |
| Your website | Homepage, service pages, location pages | Services, pricing context, expertise, FAQs | Full |
| Review platforms | Google, Yelp, Facebook, industry sites | Ratings, review text, recency | Indirect |
| Data aggregators and directories | Apple Business Connect, Bing Places, industry directories | Consistency of core business details | High |
| Local media and community | News coverage, best-of lists, chamber sites, forums | Independent validation and context | Indirect |
| Social and video | YouTube, Instagram, Facebook | Brand signals, visual proof of work | High |

Google's AI features draw on Google Search and its own data. Google's documentation says there are no special requirements to appear in AI features beyond normal Search eligibility, which means the local foundations that help you in Maps and the local pack are the same ones that help you in Google's AI responses.

Other assistants run their own crawlers and retrieval. If you want ChatGPT, Claude or Perplexity to read your site, their search crawlers need access. Check that your robots.txt and any firewall or bot-protection settings allow OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot and PerplexityBot.

## Google Business Profile: Still the Foundation

Google Business Profile is the free listing that powers your presence in Google Maps and local results. It remains the single most important local asset, because it is the structured source of truth Google holds about your business, and Google's local and AI experiences both draw on it.

### Profile completeness checklist

1. **Primary category:** choose the most specific category that describes your core business, then add secondary categories only for services you actually offer.
2. **Business name:** use your real-world name exactly. Adding keywords to the name violates Google's guidelines and risks suspension.
3. **Address or service area:** storefronts list an address; service-area businesses define service areas and hide the address if customers do not visit.
4. **Hours:** keep regular hours and holiday hours current. Wrong hours are one of the fastest ways to lose trust.
5. **Services and products:** list each service with a short, plain-language description.
6. **Business description:** write a clear summary of what you do, who you serve and where, without keyword stuffing.
7. **Attributes:** fill in relevant attributes such as accessibility, payment options and amenities.
8. **Photos:** upload real photos of your location, team and work, and keep adding new ones.
9. **Questions and updates:** keep information current and post updates when you have something worth sharing, such as new services or seasonal offers.
10. **Website link:** point to the most relevant page, which for multi-location businesses is the specific location page, not the homepage.

### Why the details matter more now

When an assistant answers "Which dentists near me take walk-ins on Saturday?", it needs a source that says you take walk-ins and are open Saturday. Profile fields and attributes are structured answers to exactly those questions. Every empty field is a question you cannot be the answer to.

## Reviews: The Language AI Uses to Describe You

Reviews are the most direct evidence AI systems have of what your business is actually like. Ratings set a threshold, but review text supplies the details assistants summarize: response time, pricing fairness, communication, specific services and specific staff.

In practice, when I look at how AI assistants describe local businesses, the phrases often echo recurring themes in reviews. A business with hundreds of reviews that only say "Great!" gives an assistant little to work with. A business whose reviews describe specific jobs gives it plenty.

### A review strategy that holds up

1. **Ask every satisfied customer, consistently.** Build the request into your process: a text or email after the job, a card at checkout, a QR code at the counter.
2. **Make it easy.** Send a direct link to your review form.
3. **Prompt for specifics, ethically.** It is fine to say "If you have a minute, mention what we helped you with." It is not fine to script reviews, offer rewards or filter who gets asked based on expected sentiment.
4. **Diversify platforms.** Google first, then the platforms that matter in your industry: Yelp for restaurants, Healthgrades for doctors, Avvo for lawyers, Houzz for contractors.
5. **Respond to every review.** Thank positive reviewers by referencing what they mentioned. Answer negative reviews calmly with a path to resolution.
6. **Watch recency.** A steady flow of recent reviews signals an active, current business.

### What to avoid

Buying reviews, review gating, posting reviews for yourself, and asking employees or family to review you all violate platform policies. Beyond the penalty risk, fake reviews tend to be generic, which means they do not help with the descriptive detail AI answers rely on anyway.

## NAP Consistency and Local Citations

A **local citation** is any online mention of your business's name, address and phone number (NAP), such as a directory listing, chamber of commerce page or industry association profile. Consistent citations help search systems confidently connect information across the web to one real business.

Inconsistency creates ambiguity. If one directory lists your old suite number, another your old phone and a third a slightly different business name, systems may treat them as separate entities or lower confidence in all of them. AI assistants that cross-reference sources have the same problem.

### Citation cleanup process

1. **Define your canonical NAP.** Write down the exact business name, address format, phone number and website URL you will use everywhere.
2. **Claim the core platforms.** Google Business Profile, Apple Business Connect, Bing Places, Facebook, Yelp and the top directories in your industry.
3. **Audit existing listings.** Search your business name, old addresses and old phone numbers to find outdated listings.
4. **Fix or remove duplicates.** Merge duplicates and correct errors at the source.
5. **Add relevant, quality citations.** Local chamber, industry associations, local business directories and supplier "find a dealer" pages.
6. **Recheck periodically.** Data aggregators can push old information back out, so check key listings at least twice a year.

Bing Places deserves special mention. Bing's data feeds a range of products, and some AI products have used Bing's search results. Many small businesses never claim their Bing listing, and it takes only minutes to do.

## Your Website: Location and Service Pages AI Can Quote

Your website is the one source you fully control, and it is where AI systems find the detail that profiles and directories cannot hold. Strong local websites have a clear page for each core service and each real location, with specific, plain-text answers to the questions customers actually ask.

### What a strong local service page includes

1. A clear heading naming the service and area, such as "Water Heater Repair in Northside."
2. An opening paragraph that states what you do, where, and for whom.
3. Specifics: what the service includes, typical timelines, what affects cost, and what customers should expect.
4. Local context: neighborhoods served, local regulations or permit considerations, and climate or housing factors relevant to the service.
5. Proof: project examples, photos and relevant reviews.
6. Practical details: hours, emergency availability, booking options and contact information as text.
7. Answers to common questions in short, direct paragraphs.

### Location pages without doorway risk

If you have multiple physical locations, each one deserves a real page with its address, hours, staff, directions, parking and what makes that location distinct. If you serve many towns from one location, resist the urge to generate dozens of near-identical city pages. Build pages only where you have genuinely local content to share. How to write passages that AI engines lift cleanly is covered in [how to write content AI engines quote](/blog/writing-content-ai-can-cite).

### LocalBusiness structured data

Mark up each location page with `LocalBusiness` (or a more specific subtype like `Dentist` or `Plumber`) using [Schema.org](https://schema.org/) vocabulary. Keep the markup identical to what is visible on the page and to your Google Business Profile.

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Plumber",
  "name": "Example Plumbing Co.",
  "url": "https://www.example.com/locations/northside",
  "telephone": "+1-555-555-0100",
  "address": {
    "@type": "PostalAddress",
    "streetAddress": "123 Main St",
    "addressLocality": "Northside",
    "addressRegion": "CA",
    "postalCode": "90000",
```

## Local Mentions, Best-Of Lists and Community Signals

Local mentions are references to your business on independent local sites: news stories, community blogs, event pages, sponsorships, and "best of" roundups. They matter in AI search because assistants answering "best" questions often lean on third-party opinions rather than a business's own claims.

When someone asks an assistant for "the best Thai restaurant in town", the assistant has to ground that judgment somewhere. Local publication roundups, award lists and frequently discussed community threads are natural candidates. You cannot control those sources, but you can earn your way into them.

### Practical ways to earn local mentions

1. **Sponsor and show up.** Youth sports, charity runs and community events usually come with a listing or mention on the organizer's site.
2. **Join and participate in local organizations.** Chamber of commerce and business association directories are legitimate citations and relationship builders.
3. **Offer local expertise to journalists.** A roofer who comments on storm preparation or an accountant who explains a local tax change earns coverage that names the business.
4. **Pursue local awards.** Many community publications run reader's choice awards; ask customers to nominate you.
5. **Partner with complementary businesses.** A realtor's "recommended local contractors" page is a meaningful mention.
6. **Be helpful in community spaces.** Genuine, non-spammy participation in local groups builds the kind of reputation people repeat.

## Common Local SEO Mistakes in the AI Era

Most local businesses that are absent from AI recommendations are not penalized; they are simply under-documented or inconsistent. These are the problems I see most often when I audit local visibility.

1. **Keyword-stuffed business names.** "Smith Plumbing – Best Emergency Plumber Northside" risks suspension and looks untrustworthy.
2. **Wrong or stale hours.** Especially holiday hours, which customers and assistants both rely on.
3. **One generic homepage and nothing else.** Without service pages, there is no specific content to cite.
4. **Ignoring non-Google platforms.** Unclaimed Bing, Apple and Yelp listings with outdated details undermine consistency.
5. **Doorway city pages.** Dozens of templated pages with swapped city names add risk without adding value.
6. **Never responding to reviews.** Silence on negative reviews leaves the critic's version as the only version.
7. **Blocking AI crawlers by accident.** Security settings that block bots keep assistants from reading your site.
8. **Hiding key facts in images.** Menus, price lists and service lists posted only as images are hard to extract.

## How to Start This Week: A Local AI Visibility Plan

The fastest gains usually come from fixing what is inaccurate and filling what is empty. This plan is designed for an owner or marketer with a few hours a day for one week.

1. **Day 1: Test the questions.** Ask Google, ChatGPT and Perplexity the questions your customers ask, like "best [service] in [area]" and "who does [specific job] near [neighborhood]". Note who gets named and what sources appear.
2. **Day 2: Perfect your Google Business Profile.** Complete every relevant field, update hours, list every service with a description and upload fresh photos.
3. **Day 3: Fix consistency.** Define your canonical NAP, then claim and correct Apple Business Connect, Bing Places, Yelp, Facebook and your top industry directories.
4. **Day 4: Start the review engine.** Set up an automated review request after every job or visit, and respond to every review from the past few months.
5. **Day 5: Upgrade one service page.** Pick your most valuable service and rewrite its page with specifics, local context, proof and direct answers to common questions, plus LocalBusiness markup.

After that, repeat Day 5 for each core service, add new photos and updates monthly, and recheck your listings each quarter. To track whether it is working beyond rankings, use the approaches in [measuring AI search visibility](/blog/measuring-ai-search-visibility).

## Be the Business Every Source Agrees On

Local SEO in the AI search era comes down to agreement. When your Google Business Profile, website, directories, reviews and local mentions all tell the same clear story about who you are, where you work and why customers like you, AI assistants have every reason to name you and very little reason to hedge.

None of it requires gaming a system. It requires accuracy, completeness and a steady habit of earning and responding to customer feedback.
