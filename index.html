Why this exists

Classic audits stop at ten blue links.

On-page tools were built to match your markup to whatever ranked in classic organic search. That race still matters. But a growing share of queries never produce a click at all — they produce an answer, assembled from pages the engine decided it could quote. Optimising for one race and ignoring the other leaves half the traffic on the table, and no classic audit will tell you it happened.

A classic on-page audit

  • Checks title, meta, H1–H6, keyword density
  • Compares word count against the ranking pages
  • Silent on whether the AI Overview cites you
  • Silent on whether the page even renders for a visitor
  • A static checklist, run once, never re-verified

Citation Gap

  • Everything a classic audit checks, against the live top ten
  • A second score for whether an answer engine can quote you

  • Renders your page in a real browser and diffs it against the source

What it measures

One URL, one keyword, six instruments.

No manual competitor research, no second rank tracker, no guessing at what the answer layer rewards. Every figure below is measured on the page as it exists right now.

01

Top-ten parityRank layer

Fetches and parses the pages actually ranking for your keyword, then scores your title, H1, word count, heading depth, lists, tables and schema against their median — a measured target, not a rule of thumb.

02

Citation readinessAnswer layer

Runs the real query against the AI Overview and People Also Ask, checks whether you are cited, and scores the things an engine actually lifts: quantified claims, attributable statistics, question headings, comparison tables and category vocabulary.

03

Rendered versus servedBoth layers

Loads your page in a real headless browser, scrolls it, settles it, and reads the computed styles. A stuck entrance animation or a script-gated counter can hide a call to action from every visitor while a text-only crawl reports it present. This catches that.

04

A costed work orderOutput

Every finding is ordered by points recovered per hour of work and split into visitor-facing defects and score-recovering tasks. Each one carries the exact numbers behind it and a prompt written to be pasted into Claude without editing.

05

Change since last scanHistory

Re-scan and each previous finding is re-checked against the fresh page. Resolved is only ever claimed when the finding's own test comes back clean — a page that merely changed is reported as changed, not fixed.

06

Confidence bandsMethod

Leave-one-out resampling across the competitor set shows how far each score could move on sample noise alone, so a two-point drift between scans is never mistaken for progress.

How it works

Four steps. No setup.

Paste a page and a keyword

The URL you want to rank, and the phrase you want to rank it for. Nothing else to configure.

It reads the live results

The real top ten and the real AI Overview, fetched fresh at scan time — never a cached rule about what Google likes.

Two scores and a diff

Where you sit on each layer, which signals fall short, by how much, and what that is costing you in points.

Work the list, re-scan

Paste each prompt into Claude, ship the change, scan again. The next report tells you what actually stuck.

The rules it holds itself to

An instrument that lies is worse than no instrument.

This tool is built to be usable in front of a paying client, which means it is built to refuse rather than to flatter. These are enforced in the code, not promised in the copy.

No estimated numbers

Every figure comes from an actual fetch, render or query. Nothing on a report is modelled, inferred or filled in.

Every number states its method

Word counts name their boundary and their source. You can reproduce any figure by hand if you want to.

Fixed means re-tested

A finding is only marked resolved when its own check re-runs clean. Otherwise it says withdrawn, and why.

It says when it cannot tell

Bot protection, a blocked fetch, a render that never fired — all reported as unknown rather than guessed.

Questions

The things people ask first.

Do I need an API key to try it?

Yes, for a live scan — a free SerpApi or Serper account gives you 250 searches a month, and a scan spends about seven of them, so roughly 35 scans. The key is stored in your browser and sent only to the search provider.

If you just want to see the output first, See a finished report loads a complete real audit with no key at all.

How is this different from Ahrefs, Semrush or Surfer?

Those measure the rank layer, and they measure it well across a whole site. This measures one page against both layers at once, including the one they do not report on: whether Google's AI Overview can actually quote you, tested by running the query.

It is a scalpel, not a suite. Most people run it on the handful of pages that matter.

Where does my data go?

The scan is orchestrated in your browser. Your key lives in this browser's storage and is never sent anywhere except the search provider you chose. Scan history is stored locally too — clearing site data clears it.

The two server functions exist only to fetch a page and to render one in a headless browser; neither keeps what it read.

Is the AI Overview result stable enough to act on?

Not perfectly, and the tool says so rather than pretending otherwise. AI Overviews are non-deterministic, so one scan is directional. Every score carries a confidence band from leave-one-out resampling across the competitor set, so you can see how much of a movement is sample noise before you act on it.

Can I put the report in front of a client?

That is what it is shaped for. Every finding prints the numbers behind it and the method used to get them, and the report distinguishes a visitor-facing defect from a score-recovering task, so the conversation is about the page rather than about the tool.

Logo'd, client-branded exports are part of the Agency tier being built.

What does it refuse to do?

Estimate. If a site sits behind bot protection, or a render never fires, or a competitor page cannot be read, it reports that page as unreadable and excludes it from the median rather than filling the hole with a guess. The sample arithmetic is printed on every report so you can see exactly what was counted.
