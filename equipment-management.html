What it covers

From a 40-ton excavator to a tool crate.

Asset tracking only works when the cheap things are tracked too — those are the ones that disappear.

Visibility

Every asset on the same map as the fleet.

Powered equipment reports through a wired tracker; trailers and non-powered assets run on long-life battery tags. Both land in the same interface as your vehicles, so nobody keeps a separate spreadsheet of where the trailers went.

  • Powered and non-powered asset coverage
  • Long-life battery tags for trailers and containers
  • Geofence alerts on yard arrival and departure
  • Last-known position retained for recovery
Utilization

Prove what you actually need to own.

Runtime and movement data show which machines earn their keep and which sit. That turns the annual rent-or-buy argument into a decision with numbers behind it.

  • Engine-hour and runtime capture on powered assets
  • Idle and dwell reporting by yard and job site
  • Utilization benchmarking across the asset class
  • Service intervals driven by hours, not calendar dates
Where it pays

Three places the tags pay for themselves.

Theft recovery

A stolen asset keeps reporting. Exact location goes to law enforcement instead of an insurance claim and a replacement order.

Hour-based service

Maintenance triggers on real engine hours, so machines are serviced when they have earned it rather than on a calendar guess.

Rental decisions

Utilization by asset shows what to keep, what to sell and what to rent for the next season.
